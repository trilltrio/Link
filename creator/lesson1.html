1Where the numbers come from

The video cites two Anthropic studies. Top users get answers of about 3,200 words versus about 600 for everyone else. Anthropic staff spend about half their week reviewing agent output, while their agents sit idle about 80% of the time.

So the slow part is not the agent working. It is you reading.

2Four parts, top-down

The video's fix is an answer style with four parts, in this order:

  1. Needs you: what only you can decide or give.
  2. Goal met? Yes, partly or no.
  3. Short path: the next few steps.
  4. Errors and why: what went wrong and the reason.

If nothing needs you, you can stop after line one. The video also mentions Claude Code's built-in concise output style as the simpler option.

Wrong: a storyI started by reviewing the files, then I updated the header, then I tried the form, which mostly worked, and along the way I noticed...
Right: top-down1. Needs you: pick the order email.
2. Goal met? Partly.
3. Short path: set email, publish.
4. Errors: none.

3Ask for visuals, not prose

The second fix in the video: when an answer is big, get it as a visual you can read at a glance. The formats it names are a one-page HTML page (like this lesson), Docs, Dashboards, Whiteboards, Sheets, Slides and Motion.
