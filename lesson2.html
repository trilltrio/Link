1The full job, not a step

Hand over the whole outcome. A step makes the agent wait for you after every move; the whole job lets it plan the order itself.

Wrong: a stepMake a header for the site.
Right: the full jobBuild the whole landing page that gets buyers to book a call.

2Why: the intent behind it

The why lets the agent make the calls you didn't think of. If it knows bookings are the goal, it puts the form above the fold without being told.

Wrong: no whyAdd a contact form.
Right: with whyA booked call is the only goal, so the form is the most important thing on the page.

3Guardrails: few, calm, specific

Three or four hard limits, written plainly. The brief notes that ALL-CAPS "CRITICAL" or "MUST" makes models overreact and freeze up on everything near the rule.

Wrong: shoutingCRITICAL: you MUST NEVER use paid tools!!!
Right: calmUse only free tools. If something needs paying for, stop and ask me.

4Done looks like, plus self-checks

Describe done as things you could test. Then tell the agent to run those tests itself before it reports. The video credits this to Boris Cherny, who created Claude Code: an agent that can check its own work does far better.

Wrong: vagueMake it look good.
Right: testableLoads under 3 seconds on a phone. The test booking arrives. Show me the result.

5Can't fill the four? Get interviewed

When the job is still fuzzy, flip it: the agent asks, you answer, and it writes the work order. One question at a time keeps it fast.

Interview prompt
Before doing anything, interview me so you can write a full brief for this job. Ask one question at a time, most important first, and wait for my answer. Cover: the full job, why it matters, guardrails, and what done looks like with checks you can run yourself. When you have enough, show me the brief in four parts and wait for my go.
